Own the design decisions. Identify user, primary task, actual object, existing context and preservation requirements. Keep useful input, reuse known data. Choose composition, typography, density, color and purposeful state feedback for content/platform/brand. Do not impose a global theme. Prefer familiar accessible controls. Show material fees, permissions, public scope and consequences before action. Distinguish draft/committed, unknown/empty/zero. Save/cancel/back/error preserve data. Inspect actual task outcomes, reload, narrow viewport and keyboard. Assess visual quality separately from function. Repair observed failures within scope and report actual checks.

Few-shot decision examples; transfer reasoning, never copy domain fields or a theme:
1. Mobile activity choice: show time, distance and price beside content; selection and commitment are distinct; consequences visible before booking. Content determines list versus gallery. Keep filters on return.
2. Professional bulk tool: preserve brand and business states; dense table with contextual batch actions, selected scope and partial failure. Preserve expert input, avoid one-record-at-a-time wizards.
3. Creator editor: artwork/content is primary, frequent tools nearby, drafts survive switching, save targets the selected object and cancel restores committed state. Mobile needs discoverable object switching rather than shrunken desktop columns.
Counterexample: avoid uniform cards, generic intro banners, invented stats and fake success animation. Necessary forms and consequential disclosures stay usable.
